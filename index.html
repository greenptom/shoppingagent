— Method —

Chicken Schnitzel

Burnt Salsa & Fennel Slaw
Prep
25 min
Cook
15 min
Total
~40 min

Schnitzel

  1. Butterfly each chicken breast: slice horizontally nearly in half, leaving the halves attached, then open out. Place between sheets of clingfilm or baking paper and gently bash with a rolling pin to an even thickness of ½–1 cm.

  2. Put the flour, eggs and crumbs into 3 separate shallow bowls. Season the flour well. Line 2 baking trays with baking paper to rest the breaded schnitzels on.

  3. Dip an escalope into the flour, shake off excess, pass through the egg, then press firmly into the crumbs to coat fully. Keep one hand dry by alternating wet and dry bowls. Lay on a lined tray. Repeat for all four.

  4. If preparing ahead, stack the breaded escalopes with a sheet of baking paper between each.

Burnt Salsa

  1. Place a cast-iron or heavy-based pan over high heat. Once hot, add the whole cherry tomatoes, onion, garlic and chilli.

  2. Cook for 3–4 minutes until everything is well-charred all over.

  3. Remove from the pan, let cool, then finely chop. Combine in a bowl with the chopped coriander, olive oil, white wine vinegar and salt to taste. Set aside at room temperature.

Fennel Slaw

  1. Mix the thinly sliced fennel with the mayonnaise, juice of 2 lemons and a generous pinch of salt. Taste and adjust salt as needed.

To Finish

  1. Heat a generous layer of vegetable oil in a large pan over medium-high heat. Fry the schnitzels until deep golden on each side, then drain briefly on kitchen paper.

  2. Serve with the burnt salsa spooned over, fennel slaw alongside and lemon wedges for squeezing.

Tip: the salsa and slaw can both be made ahead — the salsa even improves after sitting for half an hour. Fry the schnitzels last so they stay crisp.

— Method —

Mussels with 'Nduja

and Lemon, with grilled sourdough
Prep
10 min
Cook
10 min
Total
~20 min

Preparing the Mussels

  1. In a large bowl, rinse the mussels under cold water, giving them a good scrub to remove any dirt.

    Discard any with broken shells. Tap any with open shells against the side of the bowl — if they don't close, throw those away too.

The Sauce

  1. Put the olive oil and grated garlic in a cold pan (this stops the garlic burning). Bring up to medium heat for about 30 seconds, until lightly cooked but not coloured.

  2. Tip in the wine, followed by the 'nduja — break it up so it melts into the sauce. Reduce by half.

  3. Add the cream and the basil sprig (if using), then bring to a rapid boil over high heat.

Cook the Mussels

  1. Keeping the heat high, add the mussels and cover with a lid. Cook for 1–2 minutes until the shells open, giving the pan a shake after 1 minute.

  2. Remove from the heat, squeeze in the lemon juice and scatter over the picked basil leaves (if using).

Grilled Bread

  1. Set a griddle pan over high heat. Brush the sourdough with olive oil on both sides.

  2. Grill for a few minutes on each side until nicely coloured.

To Serve

  1. Spoon mussels and sauce into warmed bowls, discarding any shells that haven't opened. Serve with the toasts alongside for soaking up the sauce.

Tip: start the sauce while the bread is heating on the griddle — the whole dish comes together in about 10 minutes once you're cooking.
